\begin{abstract}
We isolate a local structural theorem about finite tetrahedral
pseudomanifold-style cores.  If a represented vertex has degree four, the
formal development constructs a $4\to1$ Pachner site.  The site is either
legal, or the opposite tetrahedron is already represented.  In the latter
case, the four source tetrahedra and the target form the five facets of the
boundary of a $4$-simplex, and the formal development proves the associated
common-face closure and vertex-link saturation statements.

The result is formalized and checked in Lean 4.  The paper is deliberately local:
it does not prove a global strict descent theorem, a classification of all
closed cores, or the Poincare conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Pachner moves are a standard local language for comparing triangulations of
piecewise-linear manifolds \cite{Pachner1991,Lickorish1999}.  In dimension
three, one of the four basic moves is the $4\to1$ move, which removes a
degree-four vertex and replaces the four incident tetrahedra by one
tetrahedron \cite{Burton2013,Regina2004}.

This paper isolates one local issue that arises when the candidate
$4\to1$ site is tested in a finite tetrahedral representation.  The
obstruction is simple to state: the opposite target tetrahedron may already
be represented.  The formal development resolves this into an exact
dichotomy and then proves a stronger structural consequence in the
target-present branch.

The emphasis here is not a new version of Pachner's theorem.  Rather, the
contribution is the formal assembly of several finite incidence arguments:
degree-four link classification, exact occurrence of the four source vertex
sets, the legal-or-target dichotomy, five-tetrahedron closure, and
propagation through represented vertex links.

The result arose in a larger program motivated by constructive approaches to
three-dimensional topology, but none of the arguments below uses the
Poincare conjecture or a global descent claim.

\section{Formal combinatorial setting}

The formal object called a \emph{triangulation} in the repository is a finite
list of tetrahedra:
\[
  K.\mathrm{tets} : \mathrm{List}(\mathrm{Tet}).
\]
A tetrahedron is a four-tuple of natural-number vertex labels.  Thus the
formal representation is list-based rather than an abstract simplicial
complex with separately stored gluing maps.

For a vertex $v$, the formal degree is
\[
  \operatorname{vertexDegree}(K,v)
  =
  \#\{\text{occurrences of }v\text{ in the represented tetrahedron list}\}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Closed triangulation core]
A \emph{closed triangulation core} is the repository predicate
\texttt{\small ClosedTriangulationCore K}.  It requires:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item every represented tetrahedron has four distinct vertices;
  \item no two represented tetrahedra have the same vertex set; and
  \item every represented nondegenerate triangular face occurs in exactly
        two represented tetrahedra.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The third condition is the usual local incidence condition for a
three-dimensional pseudomanifold.  By itself it does \emph{not} say that
the realization is a manifold: vertex links are not assumed to be spheres
or even connected.  The present theorem therefore keeps connectedness of
the represented vertex link as an explicit hypothesis.

For a vertex $v$, the represented vertex-link triangles are obtained from
represented tetrahedra incident to $v$ by deleting $v$ from the tetrahedron's
vertex list.  Two represented link triangles are adjacent when they share a
link edge.  The predicate \texttt{\small VertexLinkConnected K v} says that every
pair of represented link triangles is joined by a finite path in this
adjacency relation.

\section{The $4\to1$ site}

A \texttt{\small Move41Site} consists of five distinct vertex labels
$a,b,c,d,e$.  The center is $e$ and the four source tetrahedra are
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_0&=\{a,b,c,e\},&
S_1&=\{a,b,d,e\},\\
S_2&=\{a,c,d,e\},&
S_3&=\{b,c,d,e\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The opposite target tetrahedron is
\[
T=\{a,b,c,d\}.
\]

Two tetrahedra are identified for the purposes of the formal development
when they have the same represented vertex set; this is the predicate
\texttt{\small SameTetVertices}.

\begin{definition}[Legal $4\to1$ site]
For a site $s$ in a triangulation $K$, \texttt{\small Move41Site.LegalIn s K}
requires the following four local conditions, in addition to the closed-core
condition:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item each of the four source vertex sets occurs exactly once;
  \item the four source vertex sets are pairwise distinct;
  \item every represented tetrahedron containing the center $e$ has one of
        the four source vertex sets; and
  \item no represented tetrahedron has the target vertex set
        $\{a,b,c,d\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Thus "legal" is not being used informally.  In the formalization it means
exactly the local incidence and non-repetition conditions encoded by the
structure \texttt{\small Move41Site.LegalIn}.

\section{Incidence-two forcing}

\begin{lemma}[Incidence-two forcing]
Let $K$ satisfy \texttt{\small ClosedTriangulationCore K}.  Let $\tau\ne\rho$
be represented tetrahedra containing the same represented nondegenerate
triangular face.  If $\sigma$ is any represented tetrahedron containing
that face, then
\[
  \sigma=\tau\quad\text{or}\quad\sigma=\rho.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The closed-core incidence condition says that the filtered list of
represented tetrahedra containing the face has length exactly two.
Since $\tau$ and $\rho$ are distinct members of that list, they exhaust
its possible members.
\end{proof}

The Lean declaration is
\begin{center}
\texttt{\small ClosedTriangulationCore.eq\_left\_or\_eq\_right\_of\_common\_face}.
\end{center}

\section{The target-present cluster}

Assume that all four source vertex sets occur exactly once in $K$ and that
the target vertex set is represented.  The formal development constructs
represented tetrahedra
\[
  \tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3,\tau_T
\]
matching the four sources and the target.

It further proves
\[
  [\tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3,\tau_T].\mathrm{Nodup}.
\]

The corresponding Lean declarations are
\begin{center}
\texttt{\small Move41Site.exists\_represented\_fiveTetCluster\_of\_targetPresent},
\end{center}
and
\begin{center}
\texttt{\small Move41Site.exists\_represented\_fiveTetCluster\_nodup\_of\_targetPresent}.
\end{center}

\begin{proposition}[Finite common-face closure]
For the represented target-present cluster above, every represented
tetrahedron containing a nondegenerate triangular face of a cluster member
is itself one of the five cluster members.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The abstract five-tetrahedron configuration is the boundary complex
$\partial\Delta^4$, so every triangle has its unique partner among the
other four facets.  The closed-core incidence-two condition then excludes a
third represented tetrahedron across that face.
\end{proof}

The exact formal theorem is
\begin{center}
\texttt{\small Move41Site.represented\_fiveTetCluster\_closed\_under\_common\_face}.
\end{center}

\section{Propagation through connected vertex links}

The key local-to-star step is a propagation argument.

Let $C(\tau)$ mean that a represented tetrahedron $\tau$ belongs to the
five-tetrahedron cluster.  For a represented link triangle $\sigma$ at
$v$, define
\[
  D(\sigma)
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \text{every represented tetrahedron inducing }\sigma\text{ satisfies }C.
\]
Suppose $D(\sigma)$ holds and $\rho$ is adjacent to $\sigma$ in the
represented vertex link.  The two link triangles share an edge, say with
endpoints $x,y$.  A tetrahedron inducing $\sigma$ and a tetrahedron
inducing $\rho$ both contain the three-dimensional face
\[
  \{v,x,y\}.
\]
The finite common-face closure theorem therefore transfers membership in
$C$ across one adjacency step.  Hence $D$ is closed under link adjacency.

Since \texttt{\small VertexLinkConnected K v} supplies a finite adjacency path
from any seed link triangle to any other link triangle, induction along that
path gives $D(\rho)$ for every represented link triangle $\rho$.

This is the precise content of
\texttt{\small ClosedTriangulationCore.tetrahedronCluster\_vertexStar\_closed\_of\_commonFace\_closed}.
The concrete target-present cluster is instantiated by
\texttt{\small Move41Site.represented\_fiveTetCluster\_vertexStarClosed\_of\_connectedLinks}.

The latter theorem requires connectedness for every supported vertex link,
because it is a theorem about the vertex star of every tetrahedron in the
five-tetrahedron cluster.  The main degree-four dichotomy itself only uses
the single connected-link hypothesis at its center.

\begin{remark}[Global consequence under additional hypotheses]
The five-tetrahedron cluster is the boundary of a $4$-simplex, hence is a
triangulation of $S^3$.  If, in addition, all supported vertex links are
connected and the represented tetrahedron overlap graph is connected, the
repository's existing global saturation bridge forces every represented
tetrahedron into the five-element cluster.  Thus the target-present branch
becomes a global $\partial\Delta^4$ configuration under those additional
hypotheses.

This is a conditional consequence of the formal local-to-global
composition; it is not part of the main theorem's hypotheses.
\end{remark}

\section{Main theorem}

The principal result of this paper is the following exact formal
statement.

\begin{theorem}[Degree-four legal-or-target-present dichotomy]
Let $K$ satisfy \texttt{\small ClosedTriangulationCore K}.  Let $v$ satisfy
\[
  \operatorname{vertexDegree}(K,v)=4
\]
and assume
\texttt{\small VertexLinkConnected K v}.  Then there exists a
\texttt{\small Move41Site} $s$ such that
\[
  s.e=v
\]
and either
\[
  s.\mathrm{LegalIn}(K),
\]
or both of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item there exists a represented tetrahedron $\tau$ with
  \texttt{\small SameTetVertices $\tau$ $s.targetTet$};
  \item every represented vertex-link triangle at $v$ is represented by
  a tetrahedron whose vertex set is one of the four source tetrahedra of
  $s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The exact Lean declaration is
\begin{center}
\texttt{\small ClosedTriangulationCore.exists\_move41Site\_legalIn\_or\_targetPresent\_closes\_vertexLink}.
\end{center}

Its conclusion is, verbatim at the logical level,
\[
\exists s:\mathrm{Move41Site},\quad
s.e=v\ \land\left(
s.\mathrm{LegalIn}(K)
\ \lor\left[
\begin{array}{l}
\exists\tau\in K.\mathrm{tets},\mathrm{SameTetVertices}(\tau,s.\mathrm{targetTet})\\
\land\
\forall\sigma\in\operatorname{vertexLinkTriangles}(K,v),\exists\tau\in K.\mathrm{tets},\tau.\mathrm{linkTriangleAt?}\ v=\mathrm{some}\ \sigma\\
\qquad\land\exists\mathrm{source}\in s.\mathrm{sourceTets},\mathrm{SameTetVertices}(\tau,\mathrm{source})
\end{array}
\right]
\right).
\]

\begin{proof}
The degree-four classification first supplies a site with center $v$ and four
distinct outer labels $a,b,c,d$.  The formal certificate
\texttt{\small VertexLinkTetrahedralBoundaryCertificate} gives four represented
link vertices, four represented link triangles, and the boundary-of-a-
tetrahedron incidence pattern.  Thus the four source vertex sets have
exactly the displayed forms in Section 4.

The theorem
\texttt{\small ClosedTriangulationCore.move41Site\_sourcesExactlyOnce\_of\_vertexDegree\_eq\_four}
then proves that each source vertex set is represented exactly once.  The
outer-label coverage of the represented link implies that every represented
tetrahedron containing the center has one of those four source vertex sets;
this is the center-saturation field of \texttt{\small Move41Site.LegalIn}.

The formal theorem \texttt{\small Move41Site.legalIn\_or\_exists\_target} now has
exactly two cases.  If no represented tetrahedron has the target vertex set,
the four source-occurrence certificates, pairwise distinctness, and
center-saturation assemble a legal site.  If the target is represented, the
target witness gives the five-tetrahedron configuration.  Its common-face
closure is then combined with the connected-link propagation theorem,
yielding the stated source coverage of the entire represented vertex link.
\end{proof}
\section{What is genuinely new here?}

The existence of a $4\to1$ Pachner move is classical, and the abstract
five-tetrahedron configuration is simply $\partial\Delta^4$.  The present
paper therefore does not claim novelty for those facts.

The specific formal contribution is narrower: the repository gives a
Lean-formalized chain from a degree-four represented vertex to an exact
site-label certificate, exact uniqueness of the four source occurrences,
the legal-or-target dichotomy, a pairwise-distinct five-tetrahedron witness
in the target-present branch, common-face closure of that witness, and its
propagation through the represented vertex-link adjacency relation.

The distinction matters because the formal object is not assumed to be a
simplicial manifold.  The theorem explicitly records the incidence and
connectivity hypotheses needed at each stage rather than silently importing
topological-manifold facts.

\section{Relation to existing literature}

The ambient move theory is classical.  Pachner established that finite
collections of bistellar operations suffice to relate triangulations of
piecewise-linear manifolds in the appropriate setting \cite{Pachner1991};
Lickorish gives a detailed combinatorial treatment of simplicial moves and
the Pachner theorem \cite{Lickorish1999}.  Standard algorithmic treatments
of three-manifold triangulations and simplification include Matveev's
monograph \cite{Matveev2007}.  Regina provides a mature computational
implementation of triangulation manipulation and recognition algorithms,
including local Pachner moves and $4\to1$ moves
\cite{Burton2013,Regina2004}.

Relative to that literature, the theorem here should be read as a
formalization result about a particular finite incidence model, not as a
replacement for the classical Pachner theory.  The local target-present
obstruction is elementary once the definitions are unfolded.  What is
potentially useful for formal methods is the explicit decomposition into
small certificates and the Lean-formalized propagation from face incidence
to vertex-star closure.

\section{Global boundary}

The local theorem can be used as an input to a broader simplification or
descent program, but this paper does not define or prove a global decreasing
functional.  No global strict descent theorem is claimed, and no terminal
criterion is used to identify arbitrary closed cores with $S^3$.

Consequently, no proof of the Poincare conjecture is claimed here.

\section{Conclusion}

The degree-four branch has a precise local structure.  A degree-four
vertex with connected represented link admits a Move41 site which is either
legal or enters a represented-target branch.  The latter branch produces a
five-tetrahedron configuration with exact incidence closure and vertex-link
saturation.

This isolates a concrete, Lean-formalized local theorem from the larger
global descent program while preserving the unresolved global boundary.
