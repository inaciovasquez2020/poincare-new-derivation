\par\noindent\textbf{Mathematical correction (25 November 2026).} The averaging proof is invalid. Curvature balance does not imply either weighted degree-three incidence sum vanishes. Edges with a zero-curvature endpoint also need not lie wholly in a single sign class. Even the stated balance and existence of a degree-three edge allow all vertex degrees to equal six at the level of abstract degree data; no strict sign change then exists. This is not asserted to be a realizable triangulation. The intended manifold lemma remains unproved, and \texttt{scripts/tests/sign\_mixing\_search.py} samples unconstrained degree labels and edges rather than triangulations or curvature-balanced data. It cannot certify this lemma. See the existing unsupported-route classification in the derivation document. Historical text follows; disputed steps are not certified.\par


\section{Sign-Mixing Edge Lemma (Conditional)}

\textbf{Lemma (Conditional).}
Assume $\sum_{v}(6-d(v))=0$ and there exists at least one edge of degree $3$. Then there exists an edge $e=(u,v)$ of degree $3$ such that
\[
(d(u)-6)(d(v)-6)<0.
\]

\textbf{Proof (Averaging Argument).}
Define $K(v)=6-d(v)$ and partition vertices into $S^+=\{v:K(v)>0\}$ and $S^-=\{v:K(v)<0\}$. Then
\[
\sum_{v\in S^+}K(v)=\sum_{v\in S^-}(-K(v))>0.
\]
Assume no sign-mixing edge exists among edges of degree $3$. Then all such edges lie entirely within $S^+$, $S^-$, or $S^0$.

Let $W_3(v)$ denote the number of incident edges of degree $3$. Then
\[
\sum_{v\in S^+}W_3(v)=2|E_3(S^+)|,\quad
\sum_{v\in S^-}W_3(v)=2|E_3(S^-)|.
\]

Define
\[
\Sigma_3^+=\sum_{v\in S^+}K(v)W_3(v),\quad
\Sigma_3^-=\sum_{v\in S^-}(-K(v))W_3(v).
\]

Averaging over edges of degree $3$ gives
\[
\Sigma_3^+-\Sigma_3^-=\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E_3}(K(u)+K(v)).
\]

Under the no-mixing assumption, this forces $\Sigma_3^+\cdot \Sigma_3^-=0$, contradicting curvature balance and nonempty $E_3$.

Hence a sign-mixing edge exists.
